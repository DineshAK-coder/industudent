## The Core Problem (Why This Market is Broken)

The education industry has a dirty secret — **credentials and capability have almost zero correlation.**

A student can score 9.2 CGPA, hold 3 certifications, and still not know how to write a SQL query against a messy real-world database. And companies know this. Which is why:

- Entry-level job descriptions ask for "2 years experience"
- Hiring managers ignore resumes and test anyway
- Freshers spend ₹50,000+ on bootcamps that teach theory in a slightly more expensive way
- Companies waste 40+ hours per hire on screening rounds that could've been replaced by *watching someone actually do the work*

**The gap:** Students want proof of skill. Companies want pre-screened talent. Nobody has built the marketplace that serves both — at the right price point, with the right incentive structure.

That's the exact hole you're stepping into.

---

## What You're Actually Building

Not a course platform. Not a job board. A **two-sided project marketplace** where:

### The Student Side
- Browse real mini-projects posted by actual companies
- Pay a small attempt fee to access the full brief
- Submit their work within a deadline
- Get structured feedback from the company or your platform's reviewers
- Earn a **verified project badge** on their profile regardless of outcome
- Top submissions get flagged to the company's hiring team automatically

### The Company Side
- Post a real business problem they actually need solved (or a sanitized version of one)
- Set evaluation criteria
- Get 50–200 attempted solutions — a mix of screened talent and raw ideas
- Pay a flat posting fee
- Optionally convert top performers directly into interviews, freelance contracts, or hires

**You are not selling education. You are selling proof of work — to both sides.**

## Go-To-Market Strategy

### Phase 1 — Seed the Supply Side First (Months 1–3)

The classic marketplace mistake is launching both sides simultaneously and having neither. You go **companies first.**

- Cold outreach to 200 startups and mid-size companies with a simple pitch: *"Post a real project brief. Get 50+ attempted solutions. Pay only ₹4,999. Zero hiring commitment required."*
- Target HR leads, hiring managers, and CTOs — not procurement
- Goal: **30 project briefs live on the platform before you open student signups**
- Why this works: companies have low friction (small fee, real business value, no obligation), and having real projects makes student acquisition trivial

### Phase 2 — Ignite the Demand Side (Months 3–6)

- Partner with **10–15 college placement cells** — offer them a free institute account in exchange for promoting to their final-year students
- Run a **"Your first project free"** campaign — waive the attempt fee for first project, charge from second
- Campus ambassador program: top-performing students become evangelists, get revenue share on referrals
- Content strategy: publish *"What companies actually evaluated in submissions"* — brutally honest breakdowns that go viral in student communities (LinkedIn, Reddit, Discord)

### Phase 3 — Lock In the Flywheel (Months 6–18)

Once you have placement success stories:
- **One student placed = one press release + one LinkedIn case study**
- Each placement story brings 200+ new student signups organically
- Companies that hire once almost always post again (100% retention on hiring clients)
- Institute white-label deals become inbound as placement officers hear about you

## The Moat — Why You're Hard to Kill

**1. Proprietary Submission Data**
After 100,000 submissions across domains, you know *exactly* what separates a good data analyst from a great one, what companies in fintech value vs. edtech companies, what a ₹8 LPA candidate looks like vs. a ₹20 LPA one. No competitor can buy this. You accumulate it passively.

**2. Reviewer Network Quality**
Your freelance reviewers are industry practitioners. Over time, the best reviewers become platform celebrities. Students choose projects partly based on *who will review their work.* This is a content moat disguised as ops.

**3. Company Relationship Stickiness**
A company that has posted 8 projects, hired 3 people, and has an ATS integration isn't switching to a competitor for a ₹1,000 price difference. The switching cost compounds over time.

**4. Student Profile Depth**
A student's profile on your platform after 6 months has 8 real project submissions, company feedback scores, domain badges, and peer rankings. This profile becomes more valuable than their LinkedIn. They won't abandon it.

**5. Institute Lock-In**
Once a college's placement cell runs on your infrastructure, the next batch of students arrives pre-loaded onto your platform. You get annual recurring cohorts without spending a rupee on acquisition.

## The Risks and How You Handle Them

| Risk | Reality | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Companies post fake/useless briefs | Kills student trust fast | Editorial review of every brief before it goes live. Reject anything vague. |
| Students plagiarize each other | Degrades company value | Similarity detection + randomized brief variants per student |
| Reviewer quality inconsistency | Students feel cheated | Reviewer rating system + calibration sessions monthly |
| Companies ghost after submissions | Destroys supply-side trust | SLA commitment: companies must provide at least auto-scored feedback within 7 days or brief gets pulled |
| Big player copies you | Always a risk | Move fast on institute deals and API integrations — lock in distribution before the category gets crowded |
